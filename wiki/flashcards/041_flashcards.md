<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the genetic etiology, inheritance pattern, and hallmark clinical features of **Kallman Syndrome**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Causative Gene(s):** `ANOS1` · **Inheritance:** XLR

**Clinical & Molecular Profile:** Clinical Presentation: Classically anosmia. May have spectrum of intact sense of smell. — Genes: KAL-1/ANOS1 — Inheritance: X-linked recessive

### Card 2 (Syndrome & OMIM Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the genetic etiology, inheritance pattern, and hallmark clinical features of **Adrenal hypoplasia congenita**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Causative Gene(s):** `NROB1` · **Inheritance:** XLR

**Clinical & Molecular Profile:** Clinical Presentation: Males only, salt wasting adrenal crisis infancy, +/−under virilization, and potential for abnormal function of GnRH/LH/FSH producing cells — Genes: NROB1 — Inheritance: X-linked recessive

</details>

### Card 11 (Molecular Etiology)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> Pathogenic variants in **`GNAS1`** are associated with which clinical phenotype and molecular mechanism in **Gonadotropin Dependent Precocious Puberty**?</summary>

**Answer:**
Another example is McCune-Albright syndrome in which constitutive activity of the LH receptor (GIPP), due to a somatic activating variant of the GNAS1 gene that encodes for the cAMP G-alpha subunit of the LH receptor, causes production of gonadal sex steroids that leads to the premature activation of the HPG axis and subsequent GDPP.

</details>

### Card 13 (Molecular Etiology)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> Pathogenic variants in **`DLK1`** are associated with which clinical phenotype and molecular mechanism in **Gonadotropin Dependent Precocious Puberty**?</summary>

**Answer:**
