<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the genetic etiology, inheritance pattern, and hallmark clinical features of **CHARGE association**?</summary>

### Card 2 (Syndrome & OMIM Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the genetic etiology, inheritance pattern, and hallmark clinical features of **Meckel-Gruber syndrome**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Causative Gene(s):** `MKS1, TMEM216, TMEM67, CEP290, RPGRIP1L, CC2D2A` · **Inheritance:** Genetic

**Clinical & Molecular Profile:** Ocular Associations: Microphthalmos, anophthalmos, chorioretinal abnormalities, hypoplasia/ aplasia of iris, sclerocornea — Occipital encephalocele, microcephaly, multicystic kidney, polydactyly of fingers and toes, hepatic fibrosis, ambiguous genitalia — Causative Gene: MKS1,TMEM216, TMEM67,CEP290, RPGRIP1L,CC2D2A and others

</details>

### Card 4 (Syndrome & OMIM Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the genetic etiology, inheritance pattern, and hallmark clinical features of **Rubinstein-Taybi syndrome**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Causative Gene(s):** `EP300` · **Inheritance:** Genetic

**Clinical & Molecular Profile:** Ocular Associations: Congenital or juvenile glaucoma, coloboma, nystagmus, strabismus — Broad thumbs and halluces, short stature, DD/ID, facial anomalies, risk of cancerous and noncancerous tumors — Causative Gene: CREBBP,EP300, microdeletions of chromosome 16p

</details>

### Card 5 (Syndrome & OMIM Profile)

</details>

### Card 14 (Molecular Etiology)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> Pathogenic variants in **`ATF6`** are associated with which clinical phenotype and molecular mechanism in **Achromatopsia**?</summary>

**Answer:**
Achromatopsia demonstrates AR inheritance in most patients and pathogenic variants have been demonstrated in six genes: CNGA3 and CNGB3 which account for an estimated 80–90% of cases; PDE6C , GNAT2 , ATF6 , and PDE6H are less common.

</details>

### Card 19 (Clinical & Diagnostic Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the clinical evaluation of **Microphthalmos, Anophthalmos, and Coloboma (MAC) Disorders**, how is **Microphthalmos** defined and characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Microphthalmos represents a small eye, defined as two standard deviations less than the normal for chronological age.

</details>

### Card 20 (Clinical & Diagnostic Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the clinical evaluation of **Microphthalmos, Anophthalmos, and Coloboma (MAC) Disorders**, how is **Anophthalmos** defined and characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Anophthalmos is described as a complete absence of ocular tissues, which can be confirmed either by ultrasound or histological examination.

</details>

### Card 21 (Clinical & Diagnostic Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the clinical evaluation of **Microphthalmos, Anophthalmos, and Coloboma (MAC) Disorders**, how is **Coloboma** defined and characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Coloboma refers to a congenital defect in the uveal tissue owing to the incomplete closure of the choroidal fissure during embryogenesis.

</details>

### Card 24 (Clinical & Diagnostic Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the clinical evaluation of **Microphthalmos, Anophthalmos, and Coloboma (MAC) Disorders**, how is **Extraocular anomalies** defined and characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**

</details>
