### Card 1 (Syndrome & OMIM Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the genetic etiology, inheritance pattern, and hallmark clinical features of **SLE, infections due to encapsulated bacteria**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Causative Gene(s):** `C2` · **Inheritance:** AR

**Clinical & Molecular Profile:** Deficiency: C1q C1r,C1s C2 C4 (total) — Inheritance: Autosomal recessive — C2 deficiency is one of the most common complement deficiencies, with an estimated prevalence of 1:20,000 in the Swedish population.

</details>

**Answer:**
Interpretation of C3 and CD4 complement levels. (A) Normal C3 and C4 levels in the presence of recurrent infections should prompt the physician to analyze the functional integrity of the complement cascade. In this situation, an undetectable CH50 test indicates deficiency of a complement component. (B) Normal C3 but reduced C4 levels are indicative of activation of the classical pathway but without alternative pathway activation. Testing for C1INH and cryoglobulins, which typically give only a low C4, is indicated. If these two assays are negative and C4 levels are repetitively low, genetic deficiency of one or more alleles of C4 is suspected. (C) Low C3 but normal C4 levels suggest alternative pathway activation without activation of the classical pathway. This can occur nephritic factors (C3 nephritic factor or C4 nephritic factor), autoantibodies against complement regulatory proteins (i.e., Factor H and Factor I), or genetic defects in Factor, Factor I, MCP, C3, or Factor B. Poststreptococcal glomerulonephritis and IgA nephritis can also cause this pattern. (D) Simultaneous low C3 and C4 levels indicate significant complement consumption by immune complexes due to infections or autoimmune disease. ↓, Low; 1 , positive; 2, negative; aHUS, atypical hemolytic uremic syndrome; C1INH, C1 esterase inhibitor; GN, glomerulonephritis; GOF, gain of function; HUVS, hypocomplementemic urticarial vasculitis syndrome
