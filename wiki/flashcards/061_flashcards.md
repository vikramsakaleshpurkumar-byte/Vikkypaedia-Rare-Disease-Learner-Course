### Card 19 (Clinical & Diagnostic Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the clinical evaluation of **Antibody Deficiencies**, how is **Primary antibody deficiency (PAD)** defined and characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Primary antibody deficiency (PAD) is a group of inherited or acquired disorders that have a primary effect of impairing the body’s ability to produce antibodies, which are proteins that play an integral part in the immune system.

</details>

<details><summary><b>Q:</b> Identify the diagnostic sign, dysmorphic phenotype, neuroimaging pattern, or molecular pathway illustrated below (Fig. 61.1):</summary>

**Answer:**
Signal transduction by the B cell receptor (A) and its coreceptor complex (B) . (A) Antigen-induced cross-linking of the BCR leads to clustering and localization in membrane lipid rafts, activation of SRC family tyrosine kinases, and tyrosine phosphorylation of the cytoplasmic domains of the Ig α/Ig β heterodimer. Activation leads to docking of SYK and subsequent tyrosine phosphorylation including BTK and PI3K. (B) The BCR coreceptor complex consists of the CR2 complement receptor, CD19, and CD81. Microbial antigens that have bound the complement fragment C3d can simultaneously bind to the CR2 molecule and the BCR. Binding triggers dual signaling cascades, which result in enhanced downstream signal transduction and B cell activation. Ig, Immunoglobulin; P, phosphate; PI3, phosphatidylinositol 3; PLC γ, phospholipase Cγ. (Modified from Abbas A, Lichtman A, Pillai S. Cellular and Molecular Immunology . 9th ed. Philadelphia: Elsevier; 2018:167-168.)

</details>
