### Card 10 (Syndrome & OMIM Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the genetic etiology, inheritance pattern, and hallmark clinical features of **Dejerine-Sottas disease (DSD or CMT3/HMSN III)**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Causative Gene(s):** `CMT3` · **Inheritance:** Genetic

**Clinical & Molecular Profile:** Onset age<2 years, early foot deformities, motor global developmental delay, frequent cranial nerve involvement, relatively milder course, hypo-dysmyelination, very low NCV (≤12 m/s)

</details>

### Card 11 (Molecular Etiology)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> Pathogenic variants in **`CMT3`** are associated with which clinical phenotype and molecular mechanism in **The Main CMT/HMSN Types**?</summary>

**Answer:**
DejerineSottas disease (DSD) , (CMT3/HMSN III) includes the most severe forms of CMT1/CMT4, with onset before age 2, very low NCVs (<12 m/s), and nerve biopsy evidence of hypomyelination-dysmyelination.

</details>

### Card 12 (Molecular Etiology)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> Pathogenic variants in **`FDG4`** are associated with which clinical phenotype and molecular mechanism in **CMT4**?</summary>

**Answer:**
Peculiar abnormal myelin figures (myelin outfoldings) formed by redundant loop of excessive myelin are very typical of CMT4B1-2 and also of another demyelinating autosomal recessive subtype, CMT4H associated with variants in the FDG4 gene encoding Frabin.

</details>

### Card 13 (Molecular Etiology)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> Pathogenic variants in **`CMTX1`** are associated with which clinical phenotype and molecular mechanism in **CMTX1**?</summary>

**Answer:**
Pathogenic variants in the GJB1 gene on chromosome Xq13.1 encoding connexin-32 (Cx32) are associated with an X-linked form of CMT, CMTX1, which accounts for ~10–12% of all patients with CMT and is therefore the second most common type of CMT.

</details>

### Card 16 (Molecular Etiology)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> Pathogenic variants in **`MPZ`** are associated with which clinical phenotype and molecular mechanism in **Childhood**?</summary>

**Answer:**
Sensory symptoms like pain or paresthesias are unusual in children, especially at an early age; however, sensory ataxia is seen in some demyelinating CMT forms with onset in infancy and variable severity, such as those associated with dominant MPZ and the very rare PRX recessive variants (CMT4F).

</details>

### Card 18 (Clinical & Diagnostic Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the clinical evaluation of **Hereditary Motor-Sensory Neuropathies or Charcot-Marie-Tooth Disease and Related Neuropathies**, how is **CharcotMarie-Tooth neuropathy (CMT)** defined and characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
CharcotMarie-Tooth neuropathy (CMT) , also named hereditary motor and sensory neuropathy (HMSN), is by far the most frequent hereditary neuropathy, with a pooled prevalence across different populations of about 17:100,000.

</details>

### Card 20 (Clinical & Diagnostic Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the clinical evaluation of **The Main CMT/HMSN Types**, how is **CMT2** defined and characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
CMT2 (HMSN II) indicates the primary axonal CMT subtypes , with either AD or AR inheritance, with preserved or only mildly slowed NCVs, above the limit of 38 m/s for median and ulnar motor nerves.

</details>

### Card 21 (Clinical & Diagnostic Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the clinical evaluation of **The Main CMT/HMSN Types**, how is **intermediate CMT** defined and characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
intermediate CMT , DI-CMT for autosomal dominant and RI-CMT for autosomal recessive inheritance; the X-linked CMTX1 variety frequently demonstrates intermediate NCV values.

</details>

### Card 23 (Clinical & Diagnostic Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the clinical evaluation of **CMT4**, how is **Vocal cord palsy** defined and characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Vocal cord palsy may be seen in CMT4B1 and CMT4B2 associated with MTMR2 and MTMR13 gene variants, respectively; in the latter form glaucoma, either congenital or with later onset, is evident in ~25% of patients.

</details>
