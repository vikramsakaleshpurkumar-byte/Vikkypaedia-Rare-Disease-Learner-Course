### Card 25 (Visual & Imaging Spotter)
![](../../figures/fig_p1092_02.jpg)

<details><summary><b>Q:</b> Identify the diagnostic sign, dysmorphic phenotype, neuroimaging pattern, or molecular pathway illustrated below (Fig. 48.1):</summary>

**Answer:**
Oxidative phosphorylation. Schematic representation of the process of oxidative phosphorylation which occurs on the inner mitochondrial membrane. Electrons donated from the tricarboxylic acid cycle enter the mitochondrial respiratory chain at complex I, through the oxidation of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NADH), and at complex II through oxidation of succinate and reduction of ubiquinone to ubiquinol. A third entry point for electrons derived from fatty acid oxidation occurs via the electron transfer flavoprotein. Through a series of electrochemical reactions electrons are exchanged or shuttled via CoQ10 (ubiquinone/ubiquinol) through the complexes until they reach complex IV, where oxygen acts as the ultimate electron acceptor. As electrons move through the mitochondrial respiratory chain, electrical neutrality is maintained by the extrusion of protons at complexes I, III, and IV forming a potential electrochemical gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane, which is then dissipated through complex V, driving the condensation of inorganic phosphate (Pi) with adenosine dinucleotide (ADP) to form adenosine triphosphate (ATP), the energy currency of the cell.

</details>

Mitochondrial DNA. A schematic representation of mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA), a double stranded loop of approximately 16.5 kb in size, comprising light (L) and heavy (H) strands, encoding 13 proteins (red, blue, green, and orange), 22 tRNAs (white), and 2 ribosomal RNAs (yellow). The displacement or D-loop (cyan) is a triple-stranded noncoding region essential for the replication and transcription of mtDNA. The D-loop houses the both the light strand (PL) and heavy strand (PH) promoters, as well as the origin of heavy strand replication (OH).

### Card 27 (Visual & Imaging Spotter)
![](../../figures/fig_p1094_01.jpg)

<details><summary><b>Q:</b> Identify the diagnostic sign, dysmorphic phenotype, neuroimaging pattern, or molecular pathway illustrated below (Fig. 48.3):</summary>

**Answer:**
Germline mtDNA heteroplasmy segregation. Normal mitochondria with functional mtDNAs are blue. Defective mitochondria with abnormal mtDNAs are brown. Oocytes bear hundreds of thousands of mtDNAs. After fertilization of a heteroplasmic oocyte, nDNA replication and cell division form a blastocyst, but the mtDNAs do not replicate and are randomly distributed into daughter cells. Some inner-cell-mass cells become primordial germ cells (PGCs), which form the oocytes in the female embryos. Each PGC receives only a few functional mtDNA elements, thus generating a broad distribution of PGC mtDNA heteroplasmy levels. The PGCs replicate and increase their mtDNAs and form proto-oocytes. After hormonal stimulation, multiple oocytes mature toward ovulation. Those proto-oocytes and/or embryos with the most robust mitochondria outcompete those with deficient mitochondria and survive. If all of a woman’s PGCs receive a high proportion of mutant mtDNAs, no normal proto-oocytes or embryos are available to outcompete the mutant, and multiple affected offspring result. (Modified from Wallace DC. Mitochondrial genetic medicine. Nat Genet . 2018;50:164201649 [Fig. 1A, p. 1643].)

</details>
