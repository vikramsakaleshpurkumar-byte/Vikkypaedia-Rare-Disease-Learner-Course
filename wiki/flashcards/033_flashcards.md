### Card 9 (Syndrome & OMIM Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the genetic etiology, inheritance pattern, and hallmark clinical features of **Chylomicron retention disease (Anderson disease)**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Causative Gene(s):** `SAR1B` · **Inheritance:** AR

**Clinical & Molecular Profile:** Mode of Inheritance: AR — Malabsorbed Substrate(s): Fat — Defective chylomicron formation and accumulation in the enterocyte — Clinical Features: Steatorrhea, failure to thrive, absence of chylomicrons and reduced LDL levels in the blood; neurological symptoms in some patients

</details>

### Card 11 (Molecular Etiology)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> Pathogenic variants in **`SGLT1`** are associated with which clinical phenotype and molecular mechanism in **Carbohydrate Malabsorption Disorders**?</summary>

**Answer:**
The active transport of glucose and galactose absorption is achieved by the sodium-dependent glucose co-transporter (SGLT1), while fructose is transported by facilitated diffusion (GLUT5 transporter) from the intestinal lumen into the enterocytes.

</details>

### Card 12 (Molecular Etiology)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> Pathogenic variants in **`SI`** are associated with which clinical phenotype and molecular mechanism in **Sucrase-Isomaltase Deficiency**?</summary>

**Answer:**
The sucrase-isomaltase (SI) gene, mapped to chromosome 3q26.1, codes for sucrase-isomaltase, a heterodimeric protein with two subunits, sucrase and isomaltase.

</details>

### Card 13 (Molecular Etiology)

</details>

### Card 16 (Molecular Etiology)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> Pathogenic variants in **`SLC7A7`** are associated with which clinical phenotype and molecular mechanism in **Lysinuric Protein Intolerance**?</summary>

</details>

### Card 21 (Clinical & Diagnostic Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the clinical evaluation of **Rare Inborn Defects Causing Malabsorption**, how is **absorption** defined and characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
absorption involves the entry of the nutrient into the enterocyte at the apical pole and its secretion at the basolateral pole into the systemic circulation.

</details>

### Card 23 (Clinical & Diagnostic Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the clinical evaluation of **Fat Malabsorption**, how is **Bile salts** defined and characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Bile salts , released from gallbladder stores, assist in breaking down large fat globules into small droplets, forming micelles and solubilizing the lipids allowing enzymes to get to emulsified lipid droplets.

</details>

### Card 24 (Clinical & Diagnostic Concept)

Niemann-Pick C1-Like 1 (NPC1L1) mediates CHOL trafficking from the apical microvillus membrane to the ER.

</details>

### Card 25 (Clinical & Diagnostic Concept)

</details>

### Card 26 (Clinical & Diagnostic Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the clinical evaluation of **Other Genetic Conditions Affecting Lipid Digestion and Malabsorption**, how is **Angiopoietin-like Protein 4 (ANGPTL4)** defined and characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Angiopoietin-like Protein 4 (ANGPTL4) and ANGPTL3 inhibit lipoprotein lipase (LPL) activity through unfolding and destabilization, leading to its degradation.

</details>

**Answer:**
enteropeptidase (formerly called enterokinase), a jejunal brush-border enzyme that is released by the action of bile salts.

</details>
