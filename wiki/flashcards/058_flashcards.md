### Card 11 (Molecular Etiology)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> Pathogenic variants in **`JAK1`** are associated with which clinical phenotype and molecular mechanism in **Type I Interferonopathies**?</summary>

**Answer:**
This primarily involves the receptor-associated kinases JAK1 and tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2), which activate a heterotrimeric complex of STAT1, STAT2, and IRF9, known as interferon stimulated gene factor 3 (ISGF3), via phosphorylation of tyrosine residues on STAT1 and STAT2.

</details>

### Card 17 (Precision Therapy & Management)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What is the targeted therapeutic approach or clinical management strategy for **USP18 and ISG15 Deficiency, STAT2 - Associated Type I Interferonopathy**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Therapy / Intervention:** Targeted / Disease-Modifying Therapy

**Clinical Context:** Treatment responses to JAK inhibition have been variable, with evidence of suppression of the ISG signature without obvious impact on neuropathology although dosage may be a factor, as control of disease with high-dose ruxolitinib has been reported in a case of USP18 deficiency.

</details>

### Card 18 (Precision Therapy & Management)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What is the targeted therapeutic approach or clinical management strategy for **Clinical Management**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Therapy / Intervention:** Targeted / Disease-Modifying Therapy

**Clinical Context:** Because of the association with excessive IFN-I activity, therapeutic blockade of IFN-I signaling, primarily with JAK inhibitors (JAKi, mainly ruxolitinib, baricitinib and tofacitinib), is a cornerstone of treatment.

</details>
