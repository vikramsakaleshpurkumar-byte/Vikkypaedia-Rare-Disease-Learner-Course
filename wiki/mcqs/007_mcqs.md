**Rationale:** **Fig. 7.1**: The key structural components of the primary cilium. The cilium is a hair-like structure, protruding from the cell surface, and consists of a microtubule-based axoneme and a surrounding specialized ciliary membrane. This nonmotile cilium has nine outer microtubule doublets, but no central doublet, in contrast to motile cilia. The axoneme is assembled from the basal body, also called the mother centriole. Ciliary components, receptors, and signaling proteins are trafficked in anterograde and retrograde directions on the axoneme by intraflagellar transport (IFT) complexes (IFTA and IFTB), which engage dynein II and kinesins (blue box), respectively.
