<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: A** — Oxidative phosphorylation. Schematic representation of the process of oxidative phosphorylation which occurs on the inner mitochondrial membrane. Electrons donated from the tricarboxylic acid cyc

**Rationale:** **Fig. 48.1**: Oxidative phosphorylation. Schematic representation of the process of oxidative phosphorylation which occurs on the inner mitochondrial membrane. Electrons donated from the tricarboxylic acid cycle enter the mitochondrial respiratory chain at complex I, through the oxidation of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NADH), and at complex II through oxidation of succinate and reduction of ubiquinone to ubiquinol. A third entry point for electrons derived from fatty acid oxidation occurs via the electron transfer flavoprotein. Through a series of electrochemical reactions electrons are exchanged or shuttled via CoQ10 (ubiquinone/ubiquinol) through the complexes until they reach complex IV, where oxygen acts as the ultimate electron acceptor. As electrons move through the mitochondrial respiratory chain, electrical neutrality is maintained by the extrusion of protons at complexes I, III, and IV forming a potential electrochemical gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane, which is then dissipated through complex V, driving the condensation of inorganic phosphate (Pi) with adenosine dinucleotide (ADP) to form adenosine triphosphate (ATP), the energy currency of the cell.
